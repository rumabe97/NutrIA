## Problem

A dish is a name and four numbers. The owner wants each dish to have a photograph-like picture:
realistic, sharp, not plastic, and showing only what the recipe contains.

Half of this already exists and is switched off
([`0010`](../../decisions/0010-illustrate-recipes-not-photograph-them.md)): `RecipeIllustrator`
draws a 960 px WebP, stores it as `bytea` in `recipe_images` and serves it at
`/recipes/:id/image`, and the web shows it on the dashboard card and the meal page (#123).
Report [`0003`](../../reference/architecture/0003-imagenes-de-platos-2026-09-26.md) found
what stops it from shipping as it is:

- **Storage.** Neon's free plan holds 0.5 GB and blocks writes when it is full, so a growing
  picture library would stop plans being saved.
- **Model and prompt.** The prompt asks for "shallow depth of field", which is the blur the
  owner does not want. It also cuts the ingredients to eight, in no order, so the main
  protein can be left out while the salt is kept in.
- **Privacy.** The provider it was built for cannot be restricted to endpoints that keep
  nothing.
- **Cost.** Drawing every new dish when a plan is generated costs about 0.70–1.10 $ a plan,
  roughly 100 times the plan's text.
- **Fidelity.** Nothing checks that a picture shows only the recipe's food.

**Pilot, 2026-09-27.** 20 dishes were drawn by 3 models, 2.22 $ in total, every call served
by an endpoint that keeps nothing. The owner scored the pictures blind:

| Model | Owner's mean score | Images not returned | Time per picture | Cost per picture |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MAI-Image-2.6 | 4.17 | 2 of 20 | ~24 s | 0.028 $ |
| Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite Image | 4.00 | 0 | ~4.5 s | 0.034 $ |
| Seedream 5.0 Pro | 3.58 | 1 of 20 | ~36 s | 0.043 $ |

The owner first chose MAI-Image-2.6. They then made Gemini the principal model: it is about five times faster, which matters when the picture is drawn while someone waits on the page. It never failed, and its mean score is close to MAI's. Gemini bills a fixed 0.0337 $ per picture: on Vertex it accepts only the 1K resolution, and the prompt's length barely counts. After the `legal` review ([`docs/legal/imagenes-de-platos.md`](../../legal/imagenes-de-platos.md)) the owner dropped MAI. It documents no C2PA manifest and no watermark, so NutrIA would have to mark its pictures itself. Gemini on Vertex signs its output with C2PA and also carries a SynthID watermark. **Mini pilot.** Same day, 8 dishes, about 0.32 $. Prompt v2 asked for a bigger plate, side light and a sauce sheen. Shown at real app size, the owner scored it no better than v1: v1 averaged 4.50 and v2 averaged 4.25–4.38. The same v1 pictures that had scored 3 in the first pilot now scored 4–5, so differences this small are noise. v2's bigger plate also gets its rim cut by the card crop. Serving the picture at 512, 768 or 1200 px made no visible difference on the iPhone. The raw Gemini JPEG delivered through OpenRouter keeps Google's signed C2PA manifest and IPTC `trainedAlgorithmicMedia` metadata, 171 KB on average, while any re-encode strips them. So the project uses prompt v1 and serves the raw file.

## Outcome

- The first time anyone opens a dish's meal page, the dish gets a realistic picture. It is
  drawn in the background, stored once and shown to every later viewer of that dish,
  whoever they are.
- The picture shows the dish's main ingredients, cooked as in the recipe. The whole plate is
  in focus. It never clearly shows a food that carries an allergen the dish does not have.
- Every picture a user sees is marked as generated by AI, in the words the `legal` agent
  sets.
- Pictures live in Vercel Blob, not in the database.
- Spend is capped at 10 $ a month. Past the cap, dishes stay without a picture until the
  next month. Nothing else in the product changes.
- The feature is behind a flag and ships off. The owner switches it on in production.

## Scope

**In**
- **Lazy generation.** A meal page for a dish with no picture asks for one, and the request
  returns at once. The drawing runs asynchronously and deduplicates concurrent requests for
  the same dish. The page shows the placeholder and then the picture, with no reload needed
  once it is ready.
- **Prompt.** Version v1 from the pilot, built in code from the recipe:
  - ingredients ordered by weight;
  - aromatics, oil, salt and the like described as cooked in, never as visible;
  - sharp focus, the plate centred with a margin, a 4:3 frame.
- **Model.** Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite Image pinned to `google-vertex/global`, with no second
  model. It runs on a separate OpenRouter key with the
  no-training provider restrictions, and at most 3 attempts are made per request.
- **Judge.** A vision judge that keeps nothing (qwen3-vl on DeepInfra) rejects a picture only
  when it clearly sees an extra food carrying an allergen the dish lacks. It is calibrated
  against the pilot's 57 pictures and the owner's scores, including the dish where a generic
  "sauce" was read as milk.
- **Storage.**
  - Pictures go to Vercel Blob in the fra1 region, as Gemini's original file, untouched:
    re-encoding it would break Google's C2PA signature. The 4:3 frame is cropped for the
    meal page and the card in CSS only, never in the file.
  - No user id appears in the file's metadata or in its Blob path.
  - `recipe_images` keeps the metadata (URL, model, prompt version, size) and stops
    storing bytes.
  - The existing dev rows, 3 pictures of unknown origin, are deleted.
- **Budget.** A monthly spend counter with the 10 $ cap. The owner can read what was spent
  and how many pictures were accepted, rejected or failed.
- **Labels.** Everything here uses `legal`'s strings.
  - A visible "IA" mark on both the card and the meal-page picture, with an accessible name.
  - The caption on the meal page.
  - The privacy-policy and terms changes, published before the flag goes on.
- **Provenance check.** Before storing a picture, confirm it still carries Gemini's C2PA
  manifest, and record the result.
- **Decision.** A record that supersedes `0010`: photograph-like pictures, Blob storage and
  the providers.

**Out**
- Drawing the existing library in advance (no backfill).
- Pictures in plan rows, which the owner removed in #123.
- Pictures for the professional workspace, care plans or meal swaps.
- Other models, or letting users upload or regenerate a picture.
- Scheduling the `/cron/illustrate` sweep. The sweep is either removed or kept as a manual
  tool; the plan decides which.

## Acceptance criteria

1. **No picture yet.** Opening the meal page of a dish without a picture starts at most one
   generation for that dish, even if several people open it at once. The page answers as fast
   as it does today.
2. **Picture stored.** Once a picture is accepted it is served from Vercel Blob to every
   viewer of that dish. No picture bytes are written to Postgres after the migration.
3. **Providers.** Every image and judge call is pinned to an endpoint that keeps nothing:
   `google-vertex/global` and DeepInfra, with fallbacks off. A test proves that no
   request can go out without that restriction.
4. **No personal data in the prompt.** The prompt contains only recipe data: no user,
   profile, allergy or health data. The invariant reviewer confirms this.
5. **Retry and give-up.** If Gemini fails or returns no image, the call is retried. After 3
   rejected or failed attempts the dish stays without a picture, and a later open can try
   again after a cool-off.
6. **Allergen check.** A picture is never stored or shown when the judge clearly sees an
   extra food it can name and that food carries an allergen the dish lacks. The allergens
   are mapped in code from the food the judge names, never taken from the judge's own
   allergen list. On the pilot set the judge's decisions are
   reported against the owner's scores, and the sauce-as-milk case is no longer rejected.
7. **Monthly cap.** When the month's spend reaches 10 $, no further image call is made until
   the next month, and pages keep working without pictures.
8. **AI label.** Every picture shown carries the AI label. The card mark and the meal-page
   caption use `legal`'s strings in es-ES and en-GB. A failed picture shows the placeholder
   and is not announced to screen readers.
9. **Flag.** With the flag off, nothing is generated and the product behaves as it does today.
10. **Owner's approval.**
    - `human-verify`: the owner opens real dishes on the iPhone with the flag on and sees
      realistic pictures, true to the recipe.
    - `owner-approves`: the owner reads the spend figures after the first week.
