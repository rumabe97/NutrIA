# 0003 — A realistic picture of every dish: impact, models, cost and feasibility

> **Purpose**: the `architect` agent's answer to the owner's question of 2026-09-26: *"The next
> project I want to do is generating an image for every dish. First do an analysis of the
> impact of this, of the best and cheapest models on OpenRouter, to generate super realistic
> images that look real and stick 100% to the recipe, without ingredients that are not in the
> recipe showing up, and without the dishes looking like plastic or being blurred or anything.
> Do the analysis and the feasibility first."* It starts from what already exists (the feature
> is built and switched off), compares the image models that keep the no-training rule, counts
> what it would cost and proposes a paid pilot before anything is built.
> **Audience**: the owner and the agents. **Committed**: yes. **Maintained by**: the
> `architect` agent; once merged it is not edited — a later review is a new report.
>
> Base: branch `rewrite-missing-cues` at `e036082` (on `main` at `cd7e277`, #121).
> Every number carries its label: **measured** (where), **estimated** (with the hypothesis) or
> **unknown**. Prices and lists read from an API or a public page count as **measured** at that
> source and at that hour. While writing this report **no model call was made, nothing was
> spent and nothing was written to any database**: what was read was OpenRouter's public API
> (`/api/v1/models?output_modalities=image`, `/api/v1/models`, `/api/v1/models/{id}/endpoints`,
> `/api/v1/images/models`, `/api/v1/images/models/{id}/endpoints`, `/api/v1/endpoints/zdr`) on
> 2026-09-26 between ~17:20 and ~17:45 UTC, the pages dated in [Sources](#sources), and the
> **development** database (`Nutria-E2E`) read-only, with the hosts compared against production
> by `.claude/skills/local-probe/scripts/guard.mjs` (no URL printed). Production was not
> touched: what is said about it comes from `docs/` and from the project's memory. Prices in
> dollars, as they are published; $1 ≈ €0.85–0.95 (**estimated**).

## 1. Verdict

**Yes, on conditions. It is feasible technically and legally, and half of it is built; what
decides it is the money and the policy of when a picture is drawn, not the technology.**

The conditions:

1. Only models with a ZDR endpoint, through a **separate OpenRouter key** with a monthly cap,
   and `provider.only` limited to those endpoints on every request (§ 5.3).
2. The images **out of Postgres**, in Vercel Blob (§ 7.1).
3. A **vision judge and rules in code** that reject; three attempts and, if it does not pass,
   no picture (§ 6).
4. The **label** saying the image is generated, with whatever machine-readable mark `legal`
   says (§ 9).
5. The owner picks the **policy and the spend cap** (§ 5.4), knowing what it costs per plan.
6. A **pilot of ≤ $5** that confirms the model, the hit rate and the judge before anything is
   built (§ 13, phase 1).

- **It already exists, switched off.** Decision
  [`0010`](../../decisions/0010-illustrate-recipes-not-photograph-them.md) (2026-09-08) built a
  per-recipe illustrator (`RecipeIllustrator`), a `recipe_images` table, a public
  `/recipes/:id/image` route and the thumbnails on the web. It is off
  (`AI_ILLUSTRATIONS=false`) and only knows how to call Gemini directly, which the no-training
  rule ruled out. This project is a **v2** of that, and it needs a new decision that replaces
  `0010` on three points: **where it is stored** (out of Postgres), **who draws** (OpenRouter
  with ZDR endpoints only) and **what is promised** (a realistic photo labelled as generated,
  not an "illustration").
- **What the owner has to decide is the cost per plan.** A new plan today puts **16–18 new
  dishes** into the library (**measured**, dev, the two real generations of 2026-09-26) and
  that generation's text costs **$0.007–0.0095** (**measured**, same place, Gemma 4 31B).
  Drawing those dishes costs **~$0.70–1.10 per plan** with the recommended model
  (**estimated**, § 12): **about a hundred times the plan's text on today's route**, and
  forever, because
  [`0013`](../../decisions/0013-a-third-of-every-plan-is-fresh.md) requires a third of every
  plan to be new. (Against `0064`'s estimate, $0.10–0.15 per plan with DeepSeek, it would be
  about ten times; the measured $0.007–0.0095 is Gemma 4 31B, the current route.) At 50 plans a
  month that is **~$38–55/month**; at 20, **~$15–22/month**. There are three levers (§ 5.4):
  draw everything new, draw when the detail is opened, or draw only what is reused. It is his
  decision.
- **Primary model (a hypothesis until the pilot): `microsoft/mai-image-2.6`**, served by Azure
  on a ZDR endpoint, **$0.039 per image** of ~1 MP (**measured**, OpenRouter + Artificial
  Analysis). It is the **highest-ranked model for photorealism that keeps the rule**: 4th in
  LMArena's "Photorealistic & Cinematic" category (1331 ± 9, 6,713 votes, 2026-09-24).
  **Fallback: `google/gemini-3.1-flash-lite-image`** (Nano Banana 2 Lite) through Google Vertex,
  ZDR, **$0.034**: a different company, 13th in the same table, a SynthID watermark, and from
  the family that follows instructions best. Effective cost with retries and the judge:
  **~$0.05–0.07 per accepted image** (**estimated**).
- **The three best on the market are out under the rule**: OpenAI's `gpt-image-2`/`2.5`, xAI's
  `grok-imagine-image-2.0` and Meta's `muse-image` (7th on photo at $0.01) have **no ZDR
  endpoint at all** on OpenRouter (**measured**). Neither do FLUX.2 or Qwen Image 3. The
  owner's rule stands; its price is in § 5.2.
- **"100% faithful to the recipe" is something nobody can guarantee.** What can be built: a
  prompt assembled in code from the recipe, a **vision judge** (also ZDR) that says what it
  sees, and **code that decides**: a picture with a food the recipe does not have, or without
  its main ingredient, or looking like plastic or out of focus, is rejected; three attempts and,
  if it does not pass, **no picture at all** rather than a wrong one. The result is a **measured**
  hit rate, not a promise.
- **The images cannot live in Postgres.** Free Neon has **0.5 GB of storage per project and
  blocks writes once past it** (**measured**, Neon's pricing page): with ~1,500 photos at
  100–180 KB (150–270 MB) and ~2 MB more per plan, within a few months **plans would stop being
  saved**. They go to **Vercel Blob** (the owner has been on Vercel Pro since 2026-09-26): the
  same provider that already hosts the app, cost **~$0** at this volume (**estimated**, inside
  the Pro credit).
- **Before building: a pilot of ≤ $5.** 20 varied dishes × 3 models (MAI 2.6, Seedream 5.0 Pro,
  Nano Banana 2 Lite), with the judge, in a script that refuses to touch production and stops at
  the cap. The owner scores the 60 photos on his iPhone (~1–2 h). The pilot decides the model,
  measures the real hit rate and whether the judge agrees with him. **Nothing is spent without
  his yes.**

## 3. What is there today

### 3.1 `0010`'s illustrator

- **Who draws.** `resolveImageModel` (`apps/api/src/modules/ai/ai.config.ts:356-362`) returns
  `gemini-2.5-flash-image` through Google's API **only** if `AI_PROVIDER=google` and
  `AI_ILLUSTRATIONS=true`; with `openrouter` (production since `0064`) it returns `null` and the
  sweep does nothing. That Google branch is the route the owner's rule closed (`0064`,
  alternatives): it should disappear, not be switched back on.
- **What it asks for.** `illustrationPrompt` (`RecipeIllustrator.service.ts:105-117`): an
  overhead "home cooking" photo with the dish's name, "Visible ingredients: " and the first 8
  names, a wooden or linen table, natural light, **"shallow depth of field"**, no text, people
  or hands. Version `1.0.0` (`:25`).
- **Four defects for what the owner is asking for**, all of them in code:
  1. The ingredient names come out **unordered** (`RecipeRepository.ts:347-356`, there is no
     `ORDER BY`) and are cut at 8 (`slice(0, 8)`, `:106`). Recipes have a median of 7, a p90 of
     11 and a maximum of 15 ingredients (**measured**, dev): on the large ones, the cut can
     leave out the chicken and keep the salt.
  2. It lists oil, salt, spices and stock as **visible**: that is the recipe for the model to
     paint an oil bottle, a salt cellar or a garnish the dish does not have.
  3. It asks for blur ("shallow depth of field"), exactly what the owner does not want.
  4. It mixes languages: the prompt is English and the names come in the recipe's language. The
     catalogue has an English name for **930 of 930** ingredients (**measured**, dev,
     `ingredient_names` with `locale='en-GB'`), so the prompt can go entirely in English.
- **How it is stored.** `sharp` to WebP at 960 px wide, quality 78
  (`RecipeIllustrator.service.ts:28-29, 87`), into `recipe_images.bytes` (`bytea`)
  (`recipe.schema.ts:119-130`), one row per recipe, with `model` and `prompt_version`.
  `recipes.image_url` (`recipe.schema.ts:26`) exists and **nobody uses it** (grep across `apps`
  and `packages/*/src`).
- **When.** After every plan, 8 images in the background with `waitUntil`
  (`PlanJobRunner.service.ts:113-117`), inside the **same** 300 s invocation that already spent
  up to ~170 s generating (`AI_BUDGET_SECONDS`, `Env.validation.ts:213`); and 6 per call to
  `/cron/illustrate`, which is not scheduled (P8).
- **How it is served.** `GET /recipes/:id/image`, public, `Cache-Control: public,
  max-age=31536000, s-maxage=31536000, immutable` (`Recipes.controller.ts:13, 40`). The web asks
  for it through its own `/api/v1/*` rewrite (`apps/web/next.config.js:14-25`), and the API reads
  it from Neon. Vercel's CDN cache key carries **the deployment's unique URL** (Vercel, "Purging
  Vercel CDN Cache", 2026-09-03): every deploy of the API or the web starts with an empty cache.
  The iPhone's does last (it is `immutable`).
- **What is in dev.** 3 rows in `recipe_images`, 960×720, **37.9 KB** on average, model
  `aihorde/Juggernaut XL`, prompt version `2.2.1` (**measured**): they were not produced by the
  current code (which writes `1.0.0`) and no file in the repository names that model. They are a
  dev artefact to delete before migrating. How many there are in production is **unknown** (the
  `/admin` panel shows it as "{count} without an illustration",
  `packages/core/src/repositories/Admin/AdminRepository.ts:79-98`).

## 4. Proposal

**A v2 of `0010`'s illustrator, not a new system.** What is right is kept (one image per recipe,
shared; after the plan and never before; switchable with a variable; the label) and four pieces
change:

- **Who draws**: OpenRouter, ZDR endpoints only, a key of its own with a cap; primary
  MAI-Image-2.6, fallback NB2 Lite, to be confirmed by the pilot (§ 5).
- **What it is asked for**: a prompt built in code from the recipe, in English, with what is
  visible ordered by grams and no blur (§ 6.1).
- **Who decides whether it is good**: a ZDR vision judge describes; rules in code accept or
  reject; three attempts or nothing (§ 6.2).
- **Where it lives**: Vercel Blob, two sizes made on write, served with no function and no Neon
  (§ 7).
- **When**: only by cron with a time limit, not inside the plan's invocation (§ 8).

Alternatives rejected, with the reason that killed each:

| Alternative | Why not |
| --- | --- |
| Switch Gemini direct back on (what is already written) | The rule and Google's API terms closed that route (`0064`); AI Studio is not ZDR |
| `muse-image`, `gpt-image-2`/`2.5`, `grok-imagine`, FLUX.2, Qwen Image 3 | No ZDR endpoint on OpenRouter (§ 5.1) |
| NB2 (Gemini 3.1 Flash Image) as the primary | Twice NB2 Lite's price for 7 ranking points; it stays an option if the pilot shows its fidelity pays for it |
| Stay on `bytea` in Neon | 0.5 GB per project and going past it blocks writes (§ 7.1) |
| Cloudflare R2 | Another account and another provider seeing users' IPs, to save pennies Blob does not charge at this volume |
| `next/image` | It optimises on serve and bills per transformation; variants made on write cost nothing per view |
| Drawing after each plan in the same invocation | With a judge and retries it does not fit in the 300 s the plan already partly uses |
| A visual brief from the text model from day one | Cost and one more piece before seeing whether it is needed; it is added if the pilot shows shape errors |
| The owner reviewing every photo by hand | It does not scale past the pilot; it stays for the rejected ones |

## 5. The models

### 5.1 Candidates with image output on OpenRouter

**How a published price becomes "$ per image".** OpenRouter publishes three units
(`/api/v1/images/models/{id}/endpoints`, field `pricing`, **measured**):

- **Per image** (Seedream, Qwen): the price is the image's. In `/api/v1/models` it appears
  divided by 4,175 "tokens": their own documentation gives the example of
  `completion_tokens: 4175` → `cost: 0.04`. So 0.0000095808 × 4,175 = $0.04.
- **Per megapixel** (FLUX.2): $0.03/MP on `flux.2-pro`, which in `/api/v1/models` is
  0.00000732421875 × 4,096 tokens/MP.
- **Per output token** (Gemini, MAI, OpenAI): the price × the tokens an image occupies.
  Gemini: 1K = **1,120 tokens**, 512 px = 747, 2K = 1,680 (`gemini-3.1-flash-image`); 1K/2K =
  1,120 (`gemini-3-pro-image`); 1,290 (`gemini-2.5-flash-image`) — Google's pricing table. MAI:
  Microsoft does not publish it in what was read; **~1,024 tokens** comes from dividing
  Artificial Analysis's price per image ($38.9/1,000 images) by OpenRouter's ($0.000038/token).
  The conversion was checked both ways: NB2 Lite gives 0.00003 × 1,120 = $0.0336 and Artificial
  Analysis lists $33.6/1,000.

All at ~1 MP (1K), which is what is needed: the detail's large photo occupies 1,170 physical px
on an iPhone.

**Quality.** There is no public ranking for "food" or for "fidelity to an ingredient list". The

**An opinion (labelled as one).** For realistic, faithful food, the "LLM-family" models (Gemini,
and from what is published the MAI ones) follow negative instructions ("nothing else on the
plate") better than classic diffusion models, which tend to add parsley, lemon or garnish seeds
and sometimes paint exactly what they are forbidden. There is no public data proving it for
food; it is the first thing the pilot has to confirm or knock down.

### 5.2 The price of the rule

Without the rule, the best value would be `meta/muse-image`: 7th on photo at **$0.010**, a
quarter of MAI 2.6. And the best absolute quality, `gpt-image-2`, at ~$0.21 at high. With the
rule, the realistic floor is **~$0.034–0.039** per image: **~3–4× muse-image**. Over 1,000
images, a difference of ~$25–30. The rule stands; this only says what it costs.

### 5.3 How the rule is kept on the image route

- **The content is not personal**: the dish's name, ingredients, grams, method. Nothing of the
  person enters (P10). That means the GDPR barely touches this call, but the owner's rule is
  wider ("no provider trains on what NutrIA sends") and the privacy policy promises it. **It is
  applied all the same.**
- **`0064`'s third layer, replaced.** Since `/api/v1/images` accepts neither `zdr` nor
  `data_collection` (P12), every request carries `provider: { only: [<the model's ZDR slugs>],
  allow_fallbacks: false }`: `azure` for MAI, `google-vertex/global` for Gemini, `seed` for
  Seedream. For those models, those are **the only** ZDR endpoints (**measured**). The schema
  says `only` "merges" with the account's list: whether that is a union or an intersection is
  **ambiguous** → **hypothesis**; the pilot's first call settles it by looking at which provider
  answered.
- **The first layer**: the OpenRouter account already has training off and ZDR enforced (`0064`
  § 3). Whether that enforcement also applies to `/images` is a **hypothesis**; the substitute
  third layer covers the case where it does not.
- **The second layer: a separate key for images**, with its own guardrail: only the primary
  model, the fallback and the judge, ZDR, and a **monthly cap** of its own (say $10–30). That
  way a mistake in the image sweep cannot spend the plans' budget, and the text provider list
  (`AI_PROVIDER_ONLY` = DeepInfra, CoreWeave) is untouched.
- **A client of our own.** The SDK posts to `/images/generations` (P13); an
  `OpenRouterImageClient` with `fetch` behind `ImageClient`
  (`apps/api/src/modules/ai/clients/ImageClient.ts:10`) controls the whole body, as
  `openRouterRequest` does for text, and records the response's `usage.cost` (image billing is
  all-or-nothing: a failed one is not charged, OpenRouter's documentation).

### 5.4 When to draw: the cost decision

Every plan stores the new dishes it uses, and all of them are served at least once. Three
policies:

| Policy | Images a month | Advantage | Catch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Every new dish, when it is created** | ~15 × plans | Every meal with a photo in ~10–30 min | The most expensive: ~100× the plan's text |
| **B. When the detail is first opened** | an **unknown** fraction | Only what is looked at is paid for | The first time it is seen without a photo; the plan's thumbnails stay empty until then |
| **C. Only what is reused (≥ 2 plans)** | in dev, 63% of those served (indicative) | The library comes out with photos; a one-off is not paid for | Each plan's fresh dishes come out without a photo |

My recommendation for the friends beta: **A with a monthly cap** on the key (spend never passes
what the owner sets; once reached, photos wait for the next month), and measure before growing.
If volume rises, move to **C**, which makes the photos an attribute of the library rather than of
each plan. It is also a product lever the owner can use later (photos as part of Premium), but
that is his.

## 6. Fidelity to the recipe

### 6.1 The prompt, built in code

**The model proposes, the code disposes**, as everywhere in NutrIA (`0004`):

1. **What is seen and what is not, decided in code** from the recipe: the ingredients ordered by
   grams, with their **English name** from the catalogue. The ones that cannot be seen on a
   finished plate (oil, salt, pepper, spices, stock, water, vinegar, yeast…) go to an
   "invisible" list and are **not** named as visible. The ones that are blended or dissolved
   (soups, smoothies, sauces) are described as such ("a smooth orange cream"), not as pieces.
2. **Rough proportions**: the grams are turned into a share of the plate ("the salmon takes
   about half the plate"). Models do not understand grams; the exact amount **cannot** be
   guaranteed, and the label says so ("indicative").
3. **Shape and method**: from the name and the steps (griddled, roasted, stewed, raw). If the
   code cannot infer it reliably, a prior step with the text model already in use (Gemma 4 31B
   through DeepInfra, ~$0.001) writes a visual "brief" in JSON, and the code **reads it back**
   against the recipe, just as `methodMentions` does with methods (`LOG.md:91`): a brief that
   names a foreign food is rejected. I recommend starting without this step and adding it only
   if the pilot shows shape errors.
4. **Text**: "Only these foods are on the plate: …. Nothing else on the plate or on the table."
   One serving, one plate, a centred frame with margin (so that `NextMeal`'s 16:7 crop works), a
   45° or overhead angle, window light, **everything in focus** ("sharp focus across the whole
   dish, no bokeh"), real home-cooked texture, no text, hands, people or logos. No "shallow
   depth of field".
5. **Negatives**: none of the candidates' endpoints exposes `negative_prompt` (their
   `supported_parameters`, **measured**). They go in the text, phrased as "only this" rather
   than as a list of banned things: naming "lemon" to a diffusion model can bring the lemon. The
   pilot compares.
6. `ILLUSTRATION_PROMPT_VERSION` goes up (to `2.0.0`) and is stored with every image, as it
   already is, so that only what came from an old prompt can be redrawn.

### 6.2 The automatic judge

- **Model**: a vision one served **only** by a ZDR endpoint. Three options measured in
  `/api/v1/endpoints/zdr`: `qwen/qwen3-vl-235b-a22b-instruct` through **DeepInfra** ($0.20 /
  $0.88 per million), `google/gemma-4-31b-it` through **DeepInfra** ($0.09 / $0.34; the same
  model and provider that already generates the dishes) or `google/gemini-3.5-flash-lite`
  through Vertex ($0.15 / $1.25). Per check: **≤ $0.002** (**estimated**: ~1,300 image tokens +
  400 of text, 300 of output). I recommend Qwen3-VL 235B (stronger at recognising small things)
  with Gemma 4 as the cheap alternative; the pilot decides. Both through DeepInfra, which the
  privacy policy already names.
- **What it answers** (JSON with a schema): for each expected visible ingredient, "visible / not
  visible / unsure"; the list of **foods it sees that are not on the list**; non-food objects
  (text, hands, extra cutlery, other plates); photorealism 1–5; sharpness 1–5; "looks 3D/plastic"
  yes/no.
- **What the code decides** (thresholds in code, with tests):
  - **A hard rejection** if any food appears that the recipe does not have and that carries an
    allergen (nuts, seeds, shellfish, fish, egg, dairy, bread or gluten grain, sesame, celery,
    mustard, soy…): somebody with an allergy must not see walnuts in the photo of a dish with no
    walnuts, even though the list and the allergen gate still rule.
  - Rejection if any other extra food appears, if the main ingredient is missing (the heaviest
    protein or starch) or more than one of the visible ones is, if there is text or hands, if
    photorealism or sharpness is < 4, or if it "looks plastic".
- **Retries**: up to **3 attempts per recipe**. The 2nd carries the judge's correction ("without
  the lemon", "the rice should be visible"); the 3rd, another seed or the fallback model. With
  Gemini there is a cheaper option for the 2nd: **editing** the image to remove what does not
  belong instead of redoing it (it accepts a reference image, **measured** in its
  `supported_parameters`); the pilot can try it.
- **If it does not pass**: **no image**. The recipe is left "rejected" with the reason, with the
  bad photo not stored, visible in `/admin`, and it can be requeued. The dish shows in full
  without a photo (which is what happens today).
- **How far the judge can be trusted**: vision models fail on small things (less than ~5% of the
  image) and confuse lookalikes (quinoa and couscous, turkey and chicken). The pilot compares
  its verdicts with the owner's on the 60 photos. The threshold for accepting it: **≥ 90%
  agreement** and **≤ 5% of "the judge accepts, the owner rejects"**. If it does not reach that,
  the judge moves up a model or the owner reviews the first batch by hand.
- **Safety**: the photo is **never** the source of truth for what is on the plate. The ingredient
  list and the allergen gate are code against the database (`0004`) and depend on neither the
  artist nor the judge. If the judge is wrong, the harm is a misleading photo with its label, not
  an allergen served.

### 6.3 What fidelity to expect

**Hypothesis** (the pilot measures it): 50–80% right on the first attempt, 85–95% after three,
and 5–15% of recipes with no photo. Hardest: dishes with many ingredients, blended ones and
those with no recognisable shape. Easiest: a protein, a side and a vegetable.

## 7. Storage and delivery

### 7.1 Where

| Option | Storage | Traffic | A new account | Risk | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Stay in Neon (`bytea`)** | 0.5 GB **per project**, going past it **blocks writes** | 5 GB/month shared with dev; every cache miss is a read | no | **Plans stop being saved** | **No** |
| **Vercel Blob (public)** | Pro: usage-based, $0.023/GB-month; Hobby: 1 GB free | Hobby 10 GB free; Pro usage-based with credit | no (the same Vercel) | Low | **Yes** |
| Cloudflare R2 | 10 GB free | Egress free | **yes**; whether it asks for a card is **unknown** | One more provider seeing the IPs of whoever loads the photos | An alternative if Blob ever stopped working |

The Neon numbers: 1,500 recipes × 100–180 KB (two sizes) = **150–270 MB** (**estimated**; today
dev's 3 weigh 38 KB, but they are 960 px from another model), 30–54% of the limit, plus **1.5–2.7
MB per plan**. At 50 plans a month the limit is reached in **4–8 months**. Transfer, by contrast,
is small today: 20 people × ~35 photos × 2 fortnights × 100 KB ≈ **140 MB/month**
(**estimated**), ~3% of 5 GB; with 500 people it would be ~3.5 GB and would brush the cap that
suspends production.

On Blob: 270 MB at $0.023/GB is **~$0.01/month**; operations and traffic at this volume, **~$0**
inside the Pro credit (**estimated**: the pricing page's example assumes 5 GB, 100,000 simple
operations and 100 GB of traffic included; the table says "usage-based" and does not spell it
out). Creating the store (one operation) and setting `BLOB_READ_WRITE_TOKEN` on the API's project
are **the owner's actions**.

### 7.2 Format and sizes

- **Generate at 1K, 4:3** (~1,184×864 on Gemini per Google's table, not verified here; the rest,
  similar). Store two variants made with `sharp` on write: **~1,200 px wide** for the detail
  (90–160 KB in WebP at quality ~80, **estimated**) and **~360 px** for thumbnails (12–20 KB,
  **estimated**). WebP is enough: Safari reads it from iOS 14. AVIF would save another 20–30%
  (**estimated**) at the cost of a third variant; not needed to start.
- **Immutable paths by content** (`recipes/<id>/<hash>-1200.webp`), served straight from Blob:
  no function and no Neon. That its cache is not emptied by every deploy is a **hypothesis**
  (the Blob host is not a deployment URL, but the pricing page does not say so).
- **A machine-readable mark**: on re-encoding, `sharp` drops the metadata (C2PA, EXIF). Google's
  SynthID watermark lives in the pixels and survives; whatever MAI carries does not. `sharp`
  0.35.4 has `withXmp` (`node_modules/.../sharp/lib/index.d.ts:747`): writing the IPTC field
  `DigitalSourceType = trainedAlgorithmicMedia` into each file is the standard way of saying "AI
  generated". Whether that is enough for art. 50.2 is for `legal` to say (§ 9).
- **Performance on an iPhone**: the detail's photo is the LCP candidate; with width and height
  pinned (the CSS already sets `aspect-ratio: 4 / 3`), without `lazy` and at high priority,
  ~100 KB over 4G is of the order of 0.2–0.4 s (**estimated**). Thumbnails with `lazy`. A day of
  the plan with 5–6 thumbnails goes from 240–720 KB (the large file in each) to ~100 KB.

### 7.3 What was passed to `frontend`

Sent by message: a native 4:3 ratio with the dish centred, the two variants and their widths,
that `illustrationPath` will become an absolute Blob URL (today the web does
`${API_URL}${illustrationPath}`, `MealRow.tsx:76`), the LCP priority, and that the label on
thumbnails is pending `legal`. The layout is theirs.

## 9. Legal and product (for `legal`, who owns `docs/legal/`)

- **AI Act, art. 50.2** (a machine-readable mark on synthetic content):
  `docs/legal/analisis.md:613-618` already covers it for the illustrations, counting on Google's
  SynthID, with a deadline of **2026-12-02** for systems predating 2026-08-02. With MAI there is
  no SynthID and `sharp` erases the metadata: I propose the IPTC mark through XMP (§ 7.2).
  **Question for `legal`**: whether that is enough, and whether the v2 counts as a new system
  for the deadline.
- **AI Act, art. 50.4 and 50.5** (deepfakes; a notice "at the latest at the time of the first
  exposure"): a realistic photo of a dish nobody cooked "resembles existing objects" and can look
  authentic. The detail's visible label meets it; the **thumbnails in `MealRow` and `NextMeal`
  are seen first, with no label**. **Question for `legal`**: whether a mark is needed on the
  thumbnail or a notice on the plan screen is enough.
- **Consumer law**: NutrIA does not sell food, so the risk of misleading advertising is low;
  even so, "indicative" on the label sets the expectation (the real portion will not be
  identical). And the product principle "Never fake it" (`docs/PRODUCT.md`, principle 3) and
  `0010`'s sentence ("an invented picture of it would be the most convincing lie on the page")
  ask that the label cannot be removed. With realistic photos, that weighs more.
- **The privacy policy**: the text about illustrations stays true. What is new is **who**
  receives the recipe (Microsoft/Azure, Google/Vertex or ByteDance/Seed) and that Vercel serves
  the images. The policy's closed list of companies ("DeepInfra or CoreWeave",
  `docs/legal/textos/02-politica-privacidad.md:129`, P1-12) is about the person's data:
  **question for `legal`** whether a provider that receives only recipes has to be named, and
  whether that provider's terms carry any awkward clause (Gemini's "clinical practice" one was
  Google's API, not Vertex's).
- **`analisis.md:137-138`** says the illustrations only exist with `AI_PROVIDER=google`: that
  stops being true with the v2.
