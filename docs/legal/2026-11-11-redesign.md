## 0. The two answers

1. **The theme. No sentence of `/privacidad` has to change for the switch to ship.** Storing the
   choice on the device is exempt from consent under LSSI art. 22.2, as "strictly necessary for
   a service expressly requested": the AEPD guide names colour contrast and font size as
   examples of exempt preferences (§ 1.2). In NutrIA's hands the choice is not personal data,
   because it never reaches NutrIA, so GDPR art. 13 is not triggered (§ 1.3). Keeping it on the
   device also settles the "Alto contraste" question: NutrIA holds nothing from which a visual
   impairment could be inferred (§ 1.4). One sentence is **recommended (P3)**, not required.
   The section that lists what the browser stores already leaves out two preferences that are
   live today, and the theme would be a third (§ 1.5). The exact strings are in § 1.6. They do
   not block phase 1b.
2. **The landing pictures. Yes, but not without the conditions in § 2.8.** In short:
   - every picture keeps its own «IA» mark and its accessible name, in the prerendered HTML,
     never hidden by overlap, animation, crop or `forced-colors`;
   - every file is the original bytes, with no `srcset` variant, placeholder or conversion;
   - one visible line beside the pictures says they are generated by AI, are only indicative,
     and that not every dish has one;
   - the pictures come only from published, judge-accepted pictures of `seed` or `ai` dishes,
     each looked at by the owner;
   - no generated picture appears in the share card, the structured data or the manifest.

   **No published sentence has to change first.** `/privacidad` and `/condiciones` stay true
   only while those conditions hold: if the hero used a re-encoded file, the policy's sentence
   about the marks would no longer be true (§ 2.5).

## 1. The theme kept on the device

### 1.1 What the product will do

Taken from `0106` and the PRD, not from code, because the mechanism does not exist yet:

- **What is stored**: one short string from a closed list (`claro`, `oscuro`, `huerta`,
  `pulso`, `contraste`), in `localStorage`, on the device. No attribute means Automático.
- **How it is applied**: a static inline script in `<head>` reads the value before first paint
  and sets `data-theme` and `color-scheme` on `<html>`. The script is the same bytes on every
  page, so the public pages stay prerendered. The switch also updates the `theme-color` meta.
- **What is not done**: the choice is never sent to the server and is not an account field. It
  is not in the API or the database, and it is present for signed-out visitors as well
  (`0106` § Decision 3; PRD § Themes). The mails pick Claro or Oscuro from the mail program's
  own preference, because "the server never learns" the theme (PRD § Scope).
- **`prefers-contrast: more`**: with no stored choice, Alto contraste is selected by a CSS media
  query, which the browser evaluates. Nothing is stored and nothing is sent.

### 1.2 LSSI art. 22.2: no consent, no banner

- **The rule.** LSSI art. 22.2 (BOE-A-2002-13758, consolidated to 23/01/2025) requires consent
  to "utilizar dispositivos de almacenamiento y recuperación de datos en equipos terminales".
  The exception is storage or access "en la medida que resulte estrictamente necesario, para la
  prestación de un servicio de la sociedad de la información expresamente solicitado por el
  destinatario".
- **It covers `localStorage`.** The AEPD's *Guía sobre el uso de las cookies* (May 2024), § 1,
  p. 8, says the article reaches any "dispositivos de almacenamiento y recuperación de datos",
  that is, "cookies y tecnologías similares". So the question is real even though no cookie is
  set.
- **The exemption fits this case almost word for word.** The same guide, § 2.1.2 b), p. 12, on
  "cookies de preferencias o personalización", says that when the user chooses those features
  himself, "por ejemplo, si selecciona el idioma […], el tamaño de fuente **o el contraste de
  color entre el fondo y el contenido para mejorar la legibilidad**", they "estarán exceptuadas
  de las obligaciones del artículo 22.2 de la LSSI por considerarse un servicio expresamente
  solicitado por el usuario". The condition is that "obedezcan exclusivamente a la finalidad
  seleccionada".
- **Persistent storage is fine.** The guide adds that these preferences "no necesariamente
  tendrán que ser de sesión", because asking every visit "puede resultar molesto". There are two
  limits: use is limited to the purpose, and the choice is never used "para otros fines (por
  ejemplo, para la personalización de contenidos publicitarios) ni para elaborar un perfil del
  usuario". The same guide lists "cookies de personalización de la interfaz de usuario" among
  the exempt categories (§ 1, p. 10, following WP29 Opinion 04/2012).
- **Applied here.** A theme is exactly a user-chosen look, set by an explicit tap, used only to
  paint the page and for nothing else. **No consent and no banner are needed.** It is the same
  category as the language cookie the policy already discloses under "lo imprescindible", and
  the same reasoning as [`analysis.md`](./analysis.md) P3 on the other browser keys.
- **Three conditions keep it inside the exemption** (§ 1.7): it is written only on the person's
  explicit choice, it is read only to paint, and it serves no other purpose.
- **Informing.** The guide says that "no sería necesario informar ni obtener el consentimiento"
  for exempt storage. It recommends informing about it, "al menos con carácter genérico, […] en
  la política de cookies o en la propia política de privacidad" (§ 1, p. 10). The published
  paragraph's opening sentence already does that generically. § 1.5 explains why a specific
  line is still recommended.

### 1.3 GDPR: is it personal data, and does art. 13 apply?

- **In NutrIA's hands it is not personal data.** GDPR art. 4.1 covers information "sobre una
  persona física identificada o identificable". The string `huerta` sits in a browser and is
  tied to no account, cookie or identifier. On a shared device it describes nobody in
  particular, and NutrIA never receives it.
- **Art. 13 is not triggered.** It applies "cuando se obtengan de un interesado datos
  personales relativos a él" (art. 13.1), and here nothing is obtained: the value is written and
  read on the device by a script, and no copy reaches the server.
- **Provisional, [lawyer].** One could argue that a script NutrIA ships, writing to the person's
  own device, is "processing" by NutrIA under art. 4.2, even though NutrIA never sees the
  result. On that reading the information duty would be met by § 1.6's sentence. The practical
  effect of either reading is therefore the same, which is why this is low risk and goes to the
  lawyer only by mail.

### 1.4 "Alto contraste" and health

- **The question.** Could choosing "Alto contraste" reveal a visual impairment, which would be
  health data (GDPR art. 4.15 and art. 9)? The CJEU reads "health data" broadly: data that
  reveal it indirectly, "by an intellectual operation involving comparison or deduction", count
  too (C-184/20 *OT*, 1/8/2022, in the wording of [`analysis.md`](./analysis.md) § 1).
- **The choice is not health data in itself.** People choose high contrast for glare,
  sunlight, an old screen, or taste. The AEPD lists contrast as an ordinary readability
  preference (§ 1.2). An inference would need someone to hold the choice and link it to a
  person.
- **Keeping it on the device settles it for NutrIA.** NutrIA holds no copy, so it can draw no
  inference and has nothing to protect, disclose, export or delete. The browser's own
  `prefers-contrast` setting is visible to every site the person visits. NutrIA reads it with a
  CSS media query and does not store or transmit it.
- **What would reopen the question** is the choice leaving the device in any way: a cookie
  (sent with every request), a header, a client hint the server asks for, a telemetry or
  error-report field, an account field, the subject-access export, a log line, or an asset
  fetched only in one theme, which would show in server logs. If any of those existed, NutrIA
  would hold a datum about someone's eyesight, and this section would have to be redone,
  probably to a P2. § 1.7 turns this into conditions. Today the web app has no client
  telemetry: a search of `apps/web/src` and `apps/web/package.json` on 2026-11-11 found no
  Sentry, analytics, speed insights, `web-vitals`, `sendBeacon` or `instrumentation-client`.

### 1.5 Consistency with what `/privacidad` already says about the device

The section "Cookies y almacenamiento en tu dispositivo" (`es-ES.ts:3097-3100`;
`en-GB.ts:2946-2949`) has two paragraphs:

1. **Cookies.** Session, language, "trust this device", the known-browser cookie, and the
   passkey and Google/Apple transients. All are described as "lo imprescindible para que el
   servicio funcione, y por eso no te pedimos permiso". The theme belongs to the same exempt
   category as the language, but it is not a cookie, so it does not belong in this list.
2. **Browser storage**, as an enumeration: ticks made offline until they are sent, the
   pending-review notice, and the installed app's offline copy. It ends "Todo se queda en tu
   dispositivo."

**Finding (P3, existing, not caused by 024).** The enumeration already omits two `localStorage`
keys that are live today. Both stay on the device:

- `nutria-shopping-range-v1` (`apps/web/src/lib/shoppingRange.ts:17`): the days the shopping list
  is for. "A filter change touches no network at all" (`:13`).
- `nutria:mealSize:<answer>:<kcal>:<shape>` (`apps/web/src/lib/mealSize.ts:13,69-71`): a marker
  that the person has already answered the note about meal size. Its **key** carries the
  largest main meal's calories and the meal shape, so it is derived from the person's targets.
  It is local and never sent. That is a reason to name it carefully, not to describe its
  contents.

The `sessionStorage` flags (`arrival.ts`, `AssistantChat.tsx:131,292`) last only for the tab and
carry no choice. The source note of [`textos/02`](./textos/02-politica-privacidad.md) already
cites `arrival.ts`, and they need no naming.

**Why it is P3 and not more.** The paragraph does not say "only". Nothing in it becomes false,
none of these values is sent, and the exemption needs no specific notice (§ 1.2). It is the same
class as [`analysis.md`](./analysis.md) P3 ("no banner is needed, but they do need naming"). The
risk is an enumeration that reads as complete, and arts. 5.1.a and 12.1 GDPR do not strictly
reach it, because no personal data reach NutrIA. Adding a third preference without naming any
of them would widen the gap, so the recommendation is one sentence covering all three.

## 2. Generated dish pictures on the public landing page

### 2.1 The facts

- **Today.** A picture is drawn the first time anyone opens a dish's meal page. It is stored
  once and shown to every later viewer of that dish. There is no backfill, a monthly ceiling of
  $10 applies, and a database flag can switch drawing off (`0066` § Decision). So **most dishes
  have no picture** (PRD § The design, "Food first").
- **The file.** It is Gemini's JPEG, untouched, in Vercel Blob (`fra1`). It carries Google's
  signed C2PA manifest, IPTC `trainedAlgorithmicMedia`, and the SynthID watermark
  ([`dish-pictures.md`](./dish-pictures.md) § 2.1; `0066`).
- **How it is drawn.** `DishPicture` uses a plain `<img>`, never `next/image`
  (`DishPicture.tsx:39-45`; `lib/picture.ts:12-19`). The «IA» mark sits over the top-right
  corner with an opaque ground (`DishPicture.tsx:76`). Its accessible name is "Imagen
  generada por IA", or the `alt` says it where the picture is named (`meal.pictureOf`). The meal
  page also carries the caption "Imagen generada por IA. Es orientativa: manda la lista de
  ingredientes." (`meal.pictureCaption`).
- **Who sees it today.** Signed-in people who open a dish, and the owner's console. No public
  page shows one, and no crawler reaches one.
- **The approved sheet** (`reference/03-admin-and-landing.jpg`, "Portada pública") shows three
  rotated, overlapping, rounded tiles of dishes in the hero. They have **no «IA» mark, no names
  and no caption**, and the sheet's subtitle says "La portada pública gana **fotos**". The PRD
  already records that the built product must differ from it: "A generated picture keeps its
  «IA» mark and its accessible name […], which the sheets left out, and is served as the
  original file" (PRD § What changes from the sheets, 7).
- **The landing page** is prerendered, and it is advertising: "toda forma de comunicación […]
  con el fin de promover […] la contratación de […] servicios" (Ley 34/1988 General de
  Publicidad, art. 2, consolidated to 01/03/2023). It already shows an illustrative "preview"
  plan with example dish names and figures (`landing.preview`, `es-ES.ts:2069-2083`).

### 2.2 AI Act art. 50.2: the machine-readable mark

- **The rule.** Art. 50.2 (Reg. (EU) 2024/1689, DOUE-L-2024-81079, read 2026-11-11 in the BOE's
  XML) requires the provider to ensure "que los resultados de salida del sistema de IA estén
  marcados en un formato legible por máquina y que sea posible detectar que han sido generados o
  manipulados de manera artificial".
- **The roles do not change.** NutrIA is the provider and the deployer
  ([`dish-pictures.md`](./dish-pictures.md) § 1.2). Showing an output on another of its own
  pages is still the deployer's use.
- **The amendment.** Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 added art. 111.3, which grandfathers only systems
  placed on the market before 2/8/2026. This was read on 2026-09-27 in
  [`dish-pictures.md`](./dish-pictures.md) § 1.1 and not re-read today. The pictures are subject
  to art. 50 in full.
- **What carries the mark.** The mark lives **in the file**: Google's signed C2PA, the IPTC
  field and SynthID. The landing page complies with art. 50.2 only by serving **the same
  bytes**. Every re-encode strips the signed layer: a `srcset` or `<picture>` variant, a
  resized or cropped copy, an AVIF or WebP conversion, a blurred or low-quality placeholder made
  from the file, `next/image`, an image CDN, or an optimiser in the build
  ([`dish-pictures.md`](./dish-pictures.md) § 2.2). SynthID survives a re-encode, but on its own
  it is below the code of practice's two layers (§ 1.5, measure 1.1).
- **`data-ai-generated`.** The attribute shipped on 2026-11-11 (#359) marks **text** a model
  wrote: the recipe's title and method, and the assistant's answers. For an image it is **not**
  what art. 50.2 rests on, because the file's marks are. On the landing:
  - **The `<img>`** (or its wrapper) should carry `data-ai-generated="true"` for consistency
    with the convention. It costs nothing, and a scraper that drops the file's metadata still
    sees it. Recommended, P3.
  - **Any dish name** shown with a picture carries `data-ai-generated="true"`, as the meal page's
    title does (`plan/comida/[id]/page.tsx:98`). The names were written by a model, the seed
    library included. Whether that attribute is enough for text is H7(b) in the
    [brief](./2026-11-11-lawyer-hour-brief.md). The provisional view is that it is. Required if
    names are shown, for consistency with #359.

### 2.3 AI Act arts. 50.4 and 50.5: the visible notice, now in advertising

- **The rules.** Art. 50.4: the deployer of a system generating images "que constituyan una
  ultrasuplantación harán público que estos contenidos o imágenes han sido generados o
  manipulados de manera artificial". Art. 3.60: an AI-generated image "que se asemeja a […]
  objetos […] reales y que puede inducir a una persona a pensar erróneamente que son auténticos
  o verídicos". Art. 50.5: the information is given "de manera clara y distinguible a más tardar
  con ocasión de la primera interacción o exposición" and "se ajustará a los requisitos de
  accesibilidad aplicables".
- **A public marketing use makes the deepfake reading stronger, not weaker.**
  [`dish-pictures.md`](./dish-pictures.md) § 1.3 concluded "in all likelihood yes" for the
  in-app pictures (M18 in the brief). Among its examples of deepfakes, the Commission's
  guidelines (C(2026) 5054, as quoted there, read 2026-09-27 and not re-read) list "an
  AI-generated image of a product **in advertisement** or packaging that can affect the
  audience's perception and mislead as to the actual product appearance". The landing page is
  the advertisement. Deception is measured against "the reasonably foreseeable audience"
  (points 115–116), which on a public page is everybody: people who have never seen the app,
  its caption or its terms, older people, people with low digital literacy, and image-search
  users. **Provisional: on the landing page art. 50.4 applies with more certainty than in the
  app.** The design does not change, because the product already labels as if it applied, but
  nothing about the label may be relaxed for marketing.
- **Each picture needs its own notice.** The guidelines' point 143: "each output […] any
  natural person exposed". Three tiles mean three marks. A caption for the group (§ 2.4) is
  added to the marks, never instead of them. Point 142: something that "can be easily
  overlooked" is not clear, so a footnote or the terms do not replace the mark.
- **Timing: at first exposure.** The mark must be visible the instant the picture is.
  - **In the prerendered HTML.** `DishPicture` is a client component, but Next prerenders it,
    and the mark is rendered whenever `path` is set. That must stay true for the hero: the mark
    must not depend on hydration, a `useEffect`, an observer or hover.
  - **Never after the picture.** No fade-in of the mark after the image, no stagger that shows
    the picture before its mark, no reveal on scroll that animates them separately. The mark is
    part of the picture's own element, so it moves, rotates and appears with it.
- **Clarity: three specific risks in the sheet's composition.**
  - **Overlap.** The three tiles overlap. The top-right corner of each tile **as visible** must
    show its mark, and no tile, shadow or decoration may cover it. At 320 px the composition
    changes, and the condition holds there too.
  - **Size.** A tile may be small, but the mark never shrinks below its size on the in-app card.
  - **Themes and modes.** The mark holds 4.5:1 against its own ground in all five themes
    (include its token pair in the contrast gate, PRD criterion 5). Under `forced-colors:
    active` it keeps a visible system-colour ground and border, and it never disappears with
    the gradients (`0106` § 7).
- **Accessibility (50.5, second sentence).** NutrIA, as a micro-enterprise, is outside Ley
  11/2023's obligations ([`analysis.md`](./analysis.md) § 8). Even so, the notice must reach a
  screen reader, as § 3.2 of [`dish-pictures.md`](./dish-pictures.md) already requires.
  - **A decorative tile** (no name, as in the sheet): `alt=""`, with the mark's visually hidden
    text "Imagen generada por IA" / "AI-generated image" (`picture.aiMarkLabel`).
    `DishPicture` already does exactly this when `alt` is empty.
  - **A named tile**: `alt` = `meal.pictureOf`, "Imagen de {name} generada por IA" / "AI-generated
    image of {name}". `{name}` is that recipe's own name, in the page's language (§ 2.8, P2).
  - **Never** a marketing `alt` such as "Plato saludable de NutrIA", which would drop the notice
    for screen-reader users and for crawlers alike.

### 2.4 Consumer law: misleading advertising

- **The rules.**
  - Ley 3/1991 de Competencia Desleal (BOE-A-1991-628, consolidated to 27/12/2025),
    art. 5.1: conduct is misleading if, "aun siendo veraz, por su contenido o presentación
    induzca o pueda inducir a error", able to alter economic behaviour, about "las
    características principales del […] servicio, tales como su disponibilidad, […] su
    composición, […] o los resultados que pueden esperarse de su utilización" (point b).
  - The same law, art. 7.1: an omission is misleading, and so is information that is "poco
    clara, ininteligible, ambigua".
  - Misleading advertising is unfair competition: Ley General de Publicidad, art. 3.e. The
    TRLGDCU (BOE-A-2007-20555, **consolidated to 04/11/2026**), art. 19.2, puts "la publicidad"
    under the TRLGDCU and the LCD.
  - TRLGDCU art. 61.2: "el contenido de la oferta, promoción o publicidad […] serán exigibles
    por los consumidores y usuarios, aún cuando no figuren expresamente en el contrato", and it
    counts for conformity.
  - Art. 20 (information needed in a commercial offer) applies only when price is shown. The
    hero shows none.
- **Showing the pictures is not misleading in itself.** They are the service's real output, and
  a person who signs up will see such pictures. Three things a hero full of plated food could
  make an average consumer believe would be untrue:
  1. *That these are photographs of food somebody cooked.* The «IA» mark and the caption
     answer this. The landing's words must never call them "fotos", "photos" or
     "fotografías", in any visible string, `alt` or `title`. The terms already say "no son una
     foto del plato que vas a cocinar" (`es-ES.ts`, `terms`, the AI-content section).
  2. *That every dish in your plan comes with a picture like these.* That is untrue: pictures
     are drawn on first view, with no backfill, under a ceiling and a flag (`0066`). Under
     art. 61.2 an advertised feature can be claimed as part of the service. **This is the main
     consumer-law risk**, and the caption answers it.
  3. *That the picture shows what the dish contains.* The terms already cover this ("Son
     orientativas"), and so does the caption.
- **Provisional: with the caption the risk is low.** The marketing loses nothing it is entitled
  to say, and the product principle "Never fake it" (`docs/PRODUCT.md`) points the same way.

### 2.6 Share cards, structured data and image search

- **Open Graph and Twitter cards (firm condition).** The share card is generated by `next/og`
  (`apps/web/src/app/_shared/ogImage.tsx`), and phase 1b re-colours it. A card is shown inside
  WhatsApp, X or LinkedIn **without the page**, so the HTML mark does not travel with it, and
  that showing is an exposure (art. 50.5).
  - **Putting a generated picture in it would require** burning the mark into the pixels, which
    is a re-encode. That loses Google's signed C2PA (§ 2.2). The new file would then need
    the IPTC field ([`dish-pictures.md`](./dish-pictures.md) § 2.4), and § 2.3.3 there would
    have to be redone.
  - **Not without that work, and not without coming back to `legal` first.** Until then the
    card stays the brand card, with no dish picture.
  - **The same holds for** `og:image`, `twitter:image`, the web manifest's icons and screenshots,
    the favicon, and any JSON-LD `image` (`SiteJsonLd.tsx`, `JsonLd.tsx`): no generated
    picture.
- **Image search (provisional, [lawyer], does not block).** Today no crawler reaches a dish
  picture. On the landing page, Google Images could index the file and show it in results
  **without the overlay mark**. The C2PA and IPTC travel inside the file, but how a search
  engine surfaces them is outside NutrIA's control, and I have read no source on it.
  - **Whether the deployer's duty reaches** a third party's display is not settled anywhere I
    have read.
  - **Cheap precaution:** a `noimageindex` robots directive for those files: an `X-Robots-Tag`
    header if they are served from the web's own origin, or the page-level meta.
  - **The trade-off belongs to `seo`.** Image-search traffic for a generated plate is marginal,
    and the precaution removes the question.
  - **Recommended, not a condition**, until the lawyer answers (§ 5).
- **No sitemap image entries** for these files, for the same reason.

### 6.0 The answer in five lines

1. **An X is not the answer, and remembering a dismissal on the device is not allowed.** Folding
   is allowed.
2. **The recommended design: "full while empty, folded once it has a message".**
   - A conversation with no message shows the full warning, as today.
   - As soon as it has one, the warning folds to one line that keeps two sentences always in
     view: that it is an AI, and not to write health data. A button opens the full text.
   - A short form of the same line sits under the text box.
3. **Nothing is stored.** The state is worked out from the conversation itself. So
   `/privacidad` does not change, no version constant moves, and the sentence about browser
   choices approved on 2026-11-11 does not need a new item.
4. **Two sentences may never be hidden: the AI disclosure and the health-data instruction.** The
   rest may fold, because the person already has it (GDPR art. 13.4), it is shown in full at
   the start of every new conversation, and `/privacidad` and `/condiciones` say it too.
5. **One finding predates this question (F-W1, P2).** A reopened conversation opens scrolled to
   its end, so today's warning is already out of view when that session starts. The line under
   the box closes that gap. It does not block the fold.

### 6.1 What the product does today

- **Where the warning comes from.** `threadItems` puts the warning first in every conversation:
  new, reopened or carried on (`apps/web/src/lib/assistant.ts:124-131`). `AssistantChat` draws
  it as a `Note`, with the label "Aviso" and the full text including the `/privacidad#asistente`
  link (`AssistantChat.tsx:333-348`).
- **What the tests check.** The unit test asserts that the warning is first and appears exactly
  once (`apps/web/src/lib/assistant.test.ts:7-16`). PRD 023 criterion 12 also says: "it cannot
  be skipped… it is not something a person dismisses once and never sees again".
- **Where a conversation opens.** On mount, every conversation scrolls to the end of the page
  (`AssistantChat.tsx:128-129`).
  - A new conversation is short, so the warning is in view.
  - In a reopened thread, the warning is above the fold when the session starts as soon as the
    thread is longer than one screen. On a phone, where the owner says the warning alone fills
    most of the screen, that takes few turns (not measured). This is finding **F-W1** (§ 6.8).
  - **Nothing else on these screens says it is an AI.** The layout draws the history
    (`AssistantHistory`: `conversationsTitle`, `newConversation`, `newShort`) and nothing else.
    `assistant.intro` is not drawn by any component (searched `apps/web/src`). A reopened
    thread's visible title is the person's own question. Each answer's "Respuesta del
    asistente" is visually hidden (`AssistantChat.tsx:356`).
- **The composer.** It is sticky at the foot of the page (`.composer`, `position: sticky`). Under
  the box it has a hint line (`AssistantChat.tsx:486-488`) that the text box already names in
  `aria-describedby` (`:455`). On a touch screen the Enter hint is hidden (`.enter`, `pointer:
  coarse`), so the line shows only the message allowance.
- **What is stored in the browser.** Nothing about the warning. The component writes only one
  `sessionStorage` flag, used to put focus back in the box after the page changes address
  (`:132`, `:293`).
- **The published texts that lean on the warning:**
  - `/privacidad` § «El asistente», last paragraph (es-ES.ts:3033): «**Te lo recordamos al
    empezar cada conversación**: no escribas en el asistente enfermedades, medicación ni otros
    datos de tu salud.» In en-GB (en-GB.ts:2890): "We remind you at the start of every
    conversation: please do not write about conditions, medication or anything else about your
    health in the assistant."
  - `/condiciones`, AI-content section (es-ES.ts:3476): «No escribas en él enfermedades,
    medicación ni otros datos de tu salud…». This sentence says nothing about when it is shown.

### 6.2 The law, read today

- **AI Act, art. 50.1** (Reg. (EU) 2024/1689, DOUE-L-2024-81079, read today in the BOE's XML):
  providers "garantizarán que los sistemas de IA destinados a interactuar directamente con
  personas físicas se diseñen y desarrollen de forma que las personas físicas de que se trate
  estén informadas de que están interactuando con un sistema de IA", unless it "resulte
  evidente". As in [`assistant-warning.md`](./assistant-warning.md) § 2, row 1, I do not rely on
  that exception.
- **Art. 50.5** (same text): the information is given "de manera clara y distinguible a más
  tardar con ocasión de la primera interacción o exposición", and "se ajustará a los requisitos
  de accesibilidad aplicables". Recital 132 asks that the characteristics of "colectivos
  vulnerables debido a su edad o discapacidad" be taken into account.
- **The Commission's guidelines on art. 50.** Annex to C(2026) 5054 final, 20/7/2026. Read
  today, verbatim, from a 51-page copy published by a third party. Its title page reads
  "Approval of the content of the draft Communication". The guidelines **are not binding**. The
  points this answer rests on:
  - **33**: the information must be given "at the latest at the time of the first interaction",
    and people must be "effectively and consistently informed throughout the lifecycle".
  - **37**: one recommended technique is "first-turn greetings in chatbots that may be combined,
    as appropriate, with persistent badges or labels visible throughout the interaction". It
    also recommends placing disclosures "close to the interaction interface (e.g. near the
    input/output field)" and using "simplified wording".
  - **38**: these are insufficient on their own: a disclosure only "in terms and conditions,
    URLs, or documentation", and "unclear or ambiguous signals (e.g. generic references to
    'assistant')".
  - **39**: disclosures should be "proportionate and calibrated… to remain effective,
    user-friendly and avoid… disruptions of the user experience or habituation effects
    (so-called 'banner blindness')". **This is the authority for folding.** A block that fills
    the screen in every conversation invites exactly the blindness the guidelines warn against.
  - **40**: "a single, prominent notification before the first interaction… with a particular
    natural person is likely to suffice in most instances". But "in certain riskier contexts,
    periodic reminders and context-aware disclosures are likely to be necessary to ensure
    continued user awareness". Among those contexts it names "health advice" and "the elderly…
    and those with lower literacy".
  - **142**: information is not clear and distinguishable "where it can be easily overlooked or
    missed by natural persons under normal exposure or interaction conditions (e.g.… hidden
    under layers of menu options on an online interface…)".
  - **143**: the first interaction is also "any subsequent, first interaction… by any other
    natural person". And: "As regards the first interaction with an AI-system, the information
    should, at least, be provided once at the start of an interactive session". The copy I read
    leaves the cross-reference after that sentence blank ("mentioned in point above"), so I do
    not assume which point it refers to. The example given is "when launching a conversation
    with a chatbot".
- **GDPR** (DOUE-L-2016-80807; arts. 13.1 and 13.4 **re-read today** in the BOE's XML, the rest
  as read for this folder on 2026-11-10 and 2026-11-11):
  - art. 13.1–13.2: the information is given "en el momento en que estos se obtengan";
  - art. 13.4: no duty "cuando y en la medida en que el interesado ya disponga de la
    información";
  - arts. 5.1.c and 25.2: minimisation, and data protection by default.
- **Anthropic's Usage Policy** (effective 12/11/2026, as quoted in
  [`assistant-privacy.md`](./assistant-privacy.md) § 8.6, **not re-read**): the disclosure
  "must be provided at a minimum at the beginning of each chat session or in the product
  interface". This is a contractual duty, and it binds regardless of how the AI Act is read.
- **LSSI art. 22.2** and the AEPD's cookie guide (§§ 1.2 and 1.6 above, read 2026-11-11, not
  re-read): user-chosen interface preferences kept on the device are exempt from consent.
- **PRD 023 criterion 12**: this is the owner's own acceptance criterion, not law. A design that
  breaks it needs him to amend it.

**My reading of "first interaction" (provisional, [lawyer], § 6.10 (d)):**

- **Per person and per session, not per person once.** The duty is owed to every natural person
  (art. 50.1; point 143), at least at the start of every interactive session (point 143).
- **For this product, a session is:**
  - each new conversation;
  - each return to a stored conversation, whether on another day, after a reload, or on another
    device.
- **Because of point 40, the disclosure should also stay in view during a session.** The
  assistant answers questions about food, health is its neighbour, and the audience includes
  older people.

So the AI disclosure has to be **perceivable at the start of every session, without scrolling**,
and it has to stay in view. Showing it in full once per person is not enough.

### 6.5 The recommended design: (e), for `frontend` to build

**What is stored: nothing.** The state is worked out on every render from the turns the
conversation already has (`shown` in `AssistantChat`), never from `localStorage`,
`sessionStorage`, a cookie, the account or the API. So the server renders the right state and
nothing flashes after hydration.

**States:**

| State | When | What is drawn as the first item of the thread (`threadItems` still puts it first) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **FULL** | The conversation shown has **no turn**: a new conversation at `/asistente`; a thread whose every message was deleted; a first question that never left and is back in the box | The full warning, exactly as today: `Note`, label `Aviso`, `assistant.warning` with its link |
| **FOLDED** | The conversation has **at least one turn**. This holds from the moment the first question is added to the screen, and on every reopened thread | `Note`, label `Aviso`, then `assistant.warningShort` as a paragraph, then a button `assistant.warningMore` with `aria-expanded="false"` |
| **EXPANDED** | After the person presses the button | As FOLDED, with the button at `aria-expanded="true"`, followed by the full text `assistant.warning` and its link, inside the same `Note` |

**When it changes and when it resets:**

- **FULL → FOLDED** happens when the first question is sent:
  - no animation under `prefers-reduced-motion`, and never one that leaves the line half-drawn;
  - focus stays in the box;
  - nothing is announced, because the fold is not a status.
- **EXPANDED** lives only in React state. Every load, reload, navigation and reopened thread
  starts in FOLDED, or in FULL if there are no turns.
- **There is no state in which neither the full text nor the folded line is drawn.**

**The composer.** The hint paragraph under the box (`id={hintId}`, already in the field's
`aria-describedby`) starts with `assistant.composerNotice`, followed by the allowance and, on a
keyboard, the Enter hint. It is there **in every state**: it is the persistent label. How many lines it takes at 320 px
was not measured; whatever it takes, it wraps and is never clamped (below).

**The strings.** These are drafts, and the owner approves them (PRD 12). The folded line repeats
the published words for clause 6, so es-ES.ts:3033 stays literally true in every conversation.

| Key | es-ES | en-GB |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `assistant.warningShort` | `Hablas con una IA, no con una persona. No escribas aquí enfermedades, medicación ni otros datos de tu salud.` | `You are talking to an AI, not a person. Please do not write about conditions, medication or anything else about your health here.` |
| `assistant.warningMore` | `Aviso completo` | `Full notice` |
| `assistant.composerNotice` | `Te responde una IA: no escribas aquí datos de tu salud.` | `An AI answers you: please do not write about your health here.` |

**Variants the owner may choose without coming back to `legal`:**

- **His own wording.** «Hablas con una IA, no con una persona. No escribas datos de salud.»
  works **under this design**, because every new conversation still opens with the full text.
  Under (d1) it would not.
- **Keeping clause 7 in view.** Append `Tus alergias, en tu perfil.` / `Your allergies go in
  your profile.` to `assistant.warningShort`. R-A4 then keeps its full strength in reopened
  threads.
- **Dropping the composer line.** The fold may ship without it, but then F-W1 stays open
  (§ 6.8).

**Accessibility** (art. 50.5, second sentence; Ley 11/2023 does not bind a micro-enterprise,
[`analysis.md`](./analysis.md) § 8, but the house rules do):

- **The button.**
  - A native `<button type="button">`. Its visible text is its accessible name, `Aviso completo`
    / `Full notice`, and that name **does not change** when it opens. The state is carried by
    `aria-expanded`.
  - `aria-controls` points at the id of the full text. Any chevron is `aria-hidden`.
  - The hit area is at least `--target-min`, and the focus ring is visible in all five themes and
    under `forced-colors`. Pressing it **leaves focus on the button**.
  - The full text comes straight after the button in the DOM, so the next thing a screen reader
    reads is the text it just opened.
- **The full text when closed** is either not rendered or carries `hidden`. When open, it is
  ordinary text, the link included, never inside the `role="status"` region.
- **What a screen-reader user meets.** Reading the thread, they meet the `Note` first: "Aviso.
  Hablas con una IA… Aviso completo, botón, contraído". Going to type, they hear
  `composerNotice`, because the box is described by the hint. Both duties reach them without
  opening anything.
- **Distinguishable** (art. 50.5; point 142): the folded `Note` still reads as NutrIA's notice,
  with its `Aviso` label and its own style, and never as one of the model's answers
  ([`assistant-warning.md`](./assistant-warning.md) § 1).
- **Contrast.** The folded line, the button and `composerNotice` hold **4.5:1** in all five
  themes. The hint today uses `--foreground-02` at `--font-size-01`, so put that pair in the
  contrast gate.
- **Width.** The line **wraps; it is never clamped**: no `line-clamp`, no `text-overflow:
  ellipsis`, no `white-space: nowrap`, no `max-height` with `overflow: hidden`, at 320, 390 and
  1280 px, in both languages. A clipped "No escribas aquí enfermedades, medi…" is not "clara".
  The owner's own rule says the same: nothing overflows.

**What `tests` should assert** (the owner amends PRD 12's test sentence only if he wants to; the
design keeps it true):

- the warning is still first in `threadItems`;
- with zero turns, the full text and its link are rendered;
- with one turn or more:
  - `warningShort` is rendered;
  - the full text is not visible;
  - the button reads `aria-expanded="false"`, then `"true"` when pressed, and the full text and
    its link appear;
- **no write to `localStorage`, `sessionStorage` or `document.cookie`** happens when it folds or
  when the button is pressed;
- the hint carries `composerNotice` in both languages;
- the dictionaries have the same keys in both languages.

### 6.7 If something were stored on the device after all

This applies only to (b), (c2) and (d2), which are not recommended.

- **Consent.** A dismissal the person chooses with an X is a user-chosen interface preference,
  exempt under LSSI art. 22.2 by the AEPD guide's § 2.1.2 b), as the theme is (§ 1.2). A flag
  written **automatically** ((c2), (d2)) is not something "el propio usuario elige". It probably
  still falls under "personalización de la interfaz de usuario" (guide § 1, p. 10), but that is
  provisional **[lawyer]**.
- **`/privacidad`.** The 2026-11-11 sentence (es-ES.ts:3100) lists the choices kept "en ese
  navegador", and reads as complete. A fourth key would need a fourth item, for example «y si ya
  viste el aviso del asistente» (P3, the same class as § 1.5). `privacy.updated` would move.
- **The duty itself.** None of this cures (b). Storing it legally does not make removing the
  disclosure lawful.

### 6.8 What a returning conversation needs, and finding F-W1

- **A stored thread reopened days later is a new interactive session** (point 143, provisional
  **[lawyer]**). At its start, the AI disclosure and the health instruction have to be
  **perceivable without scrolling** (point 142). The answers are already screened again against
  today's allergies (`[id]/page.tsx`, the API), and nothing about that changes.
- **Under (e)** the thread opens at its end, as a chat does. `composerNotice` is in view under
  the box, and the folded `Note` is at the top for anyone who scrolls up or reads in order. The
  expanded state is never carried over, and if every turn was deleted, the thread shows FULL.
- **F-W1 · P2, it exists today, and it is not caused by this question.**
  - **What happens.** `AssistantChat.tsx:128-129` scrolls every conversation to its end when it
    mounts. In a reopened thread, the warning, today the **only** carrier of the AI disclosure
    and of the health instruction, is above the fold when that session starts.
  - **What it touches.** Points 142 and 143 ("easily… missed", "at the start of an interactive
    session") and Anthropic's "beginning of each chat session". It is P2 rather than P1
    because:
    - the disclosure is in the page and was given at the start of the conversation;
    - the reading of "session" is provisional.
  - **The smallest change**: `assistant.composerNotice` in the hint line (§ 6.5). It does not
    move the scroll.
  - **It does not block the fold.** Folding alone is no worse than today: a reopened thread is
    already out of view. The two ship best together.

### 6.9 What must not be done

1. **No X that removes the warning beyond the conversation**, and nothing remembered on the
   device, the account or the server that suppresses the AI disclosure or the health instruction
   in a later conversation, for any length of time, "until the text changes" included. Design
   (b).
2. **Clause 1 (IA / AI) and clause 6 (health) are never behind a tap, a hover, a tooltip, a
   menu or a scroll.** The folded line says "IA" in words, not "Asistente" alone and not only an
   icon (points 38 and 142).
3. **No clamping, ellipsis or clipping** of the folded line or of `composerNotice`, at any width
   or in either language.
4. **The full text is never folded before the conversation has a message.** A new conversation
   always opens in FULL.
5. **None of it goes in the live region**, and the fold is not announced.
6. **The full text keeps its words and its link.** A change to `assistant.warning` comes back to
   `legal` and is recorded in [`assistant-warning.md`](./assistant-warning.md). The folded line is
   not a new version of it.
7. **The folded `Note` is not restyled as an answer**, and keeps its `Aviso` label.
